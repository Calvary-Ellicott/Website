Serve
Made to be given away
“For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve.” Mark 10:45

Jesus did not come to be waited on. He washed feet, fed crowds, and gave His life. When we serve, we are not doing the church a favor. We are following Him.

Peter puts it simply: each of us has received a gift, and it was given so that it could be used for others (1 Peter 4:10). Nobody at Calvary Ellicott is a spectator. The person who greets at the door, the one who runs the sound board, the one who holds a baby so a tired mom can hear a sermon, the one who shows up early to make coffee: every one of them is doing the work of the church.

Serving does something for the people you serve. It also does something for you. It pulls you out of the back row and into the family. You learn names. People learn yours. You find out that you have something to offer, and that God can use it. Most of the friendships that last in a church start on a team, not in a pew.

You do not need experience, and you do not need to be ready. You need an hour on a Sunday or an evening in the month, and a willingness to say yes.

Pick a team below. Someone from that team will reach out, show you what they do, and let you try it with no pressure.
